# Student-data privacy and compliance checklist

> **This is research input, not legal advice.** Nothing in this document is a legal conclusion, and it does not draft any Terms of Service or Privacy Policy (that is a lawyer's job, task M4-L). It is a sourced summary of the current state of Bangladeshi law relevant to this product, a data inventory, an action checklist, and a list of questions to take to a lawyer.

Access date for every legal source below: 2026-09-23.

## 1. Current status of relevant Bangladeshi law

### 1.1 Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (the main one)
- **Status: enacted, in force.** The Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 ("PDPA", Law 63 of 2026) was passed by Parliament and became effective **10 April 2026**, per a regulatory tracker (https://www.consentstack.io/regulations/bd-pdpo — 2026-09-23) and a compliance-vendor overview (https://securiti.ai/bangladesh-personal-data-protection-act-overview/ — 2026-09-23).
- **How it got there (sources partly disagree on exact dates, noted below):** a cabinet-approved draft existed from November 2023; the interim government's **Personal Data Protection Ordinance, 2025** was approved by the Council of Advisers on either 9 October 2025 (per https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/government/teeopu4dfv) or presented to cabinet 15 July 2025 per an earlier report (https://ppc.land/bangladesh-finalizes-comprehensive-data-protection-ordinance-draft/) — the two sources describe different steps in the same process and are not necessarily contradictory, but the exact date of "approval" varies by source. It was gazetted 6 November 2025, amended by the Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 on 5 February 2026, then replaced by the permanent Act in **April 2026**. (All URLs above, 2026-09-23.)
- **Enforcement body:** a National Data Governance Authority (also referred to as "National Data Management Authority" in some coverage) enforces the Act. At least one Bangladeshi newspaper opinion piece (https://observerbd.com/news/578852 — dated in-page "15 June 2026", accessed 2026-09-23) raises a concern that this authority sits under the Prime Minister's Office and may lack independent oversight power — this is commentary/opinion, not a settled legal fact, and is presented here as such.
- **Consent model:** opt-in / explicit consent required before collection, storage, transfer or use of personal data (multiple sources above agree on this point).
- **Children/minors:** the ordinance/Act requires **consent from a parent or legal guardian** to process a child's or minor's data, and explicitly **prohibits profiling, behavioural tracking, or targeted advertising directed at minors** (https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/government/teeopu4dfv and https://www.tbsnews.net/node/1281356 — both 2026-09-23). A related (still-draft, per one 2023-era legal-research PDF) definition puts "child" at under 18 (https://www.globalchildexploitationpolicy.org/... — 2026-09-23); this product's students are minors under that definition for essentially all K-12/madrasa/coaching use cases.
- **Cross-border data transfer / localisation:** at least one civil-society legal analysis states the draft ordinance required data "critical to national security" to be stored within Bangladesh, while other data may be transferred cross-border only under conditions (https://techglobalinstitute.com/announcements/... — 2026-09-23). **This is directly relevant to D-10** (hosting region closest to Bangladesh, not necessarily inside it) and to Supabase's actual hosting location — **exact criteria for what counts as "critical" data were not found in this search and are `unknown`; this is the single most important open legal question for this product and is listed first under "Questions for a lawyer" below.**
- **Breach notification:** a July 2026 news report citing Bangladeshi investigative journalism states that the enacted PDPA **contains no mandatory breach-notification requirement**, illustrated by a real incident (a supermarket chain's ransomware breach of ~4 million customers' data with no legal obligation to notify those affected) (https://ianslive.in/bangladeshs-data-protection-law-lacks-power-to-protect-citizen-data-report--20260708201319 — 2026-09-23). **This means our own breach-response commitment (checklist §3) is a voluntary/contractual best practice in Bangladesh, not something the law currently forces us to do** — worth stating plainly to the Owner rather than assuming the law covers this.
- **Penalties:** administrative fines, compensation and criminal penalties for unauthorised access, misuse or illegal processing (multiple sources); one civil-society source states the draft ordinance proposed penalties up to 7 years' imprisonment and BDT 2,000,000 fine for some violations, and argues this is disproportionate compared to Bangladesh's general sentencing norms (https://techglobalinstitute.com/... — 2026-09-23, an advocacy-group opinion, not the Act's final text, which this task's search did not directly retrieve).

### 1.2 Cyber Security Act, 2023 (replaced the Digital Security Act, 2018)
- **Status: enacted, in force since 2023-10-07** (Act No. 21 of 2023). (https://jnyross--99173f686fd611f18a001607ee4eb77e.web.val.run/laws/LAW-BD-DSA-002 — a legal-tracking database, marked secondary — and https://www.dataguidance.com/news/bangladesh-government-enacts-cybersecurity-act-2023 — 2026-09-23.)
- This is primarily a cybercrime/content-offence law (unauthorised access, data destruction, content offences under sections 25–32), not a data-protection law per se. It carries forward most of the predecessor DSA's offences with reduced penalties for some sections (https://admin.mondaq.com/security/1514296/... — 2026-09-23). Relevant to us mainly as: (a) a backdrop making unauthorised access to our systems a criminal matter, which supports our own security posture, and (b) a general caution around any user-generated content (e.g. notices) that could be construed as a content offence — flagged as a lawyer question, not something this task can assess.

### 1.3 BTRC rules on SMS content
- A BTRC notice dated **3 January 2019** (sharok 14.32.0000.600.36.001.19.51) requires that **promotional SMS be sent in Bengali** — sourced already in `providers.md` from a Bangladeshi SMS vendor's own page citing the notice (https://www.mimsms.com/ — 2026-09-23, secondary in the sense that it's a vendor's citation of the notice, not the notice itself). Relevant to the text-alert opt-out and content checklist items below; the exact split between "promotional" and "transactional" (attendance/fee alerts are arguably transactional, not promotional) is **unknown** from this search and should be confirmed.

### 1.4 Payment/financial data
- Bangladesh Bank licenses Payment Service Operators/Providers (PSO/PSP); at least one of the payment gateways reviewed in `providers.md` (SSLCommerz) states it is "Bangladesh Bank PSO Licensed" (https://bengalcloud.com/best-payment-gateway-in-bangladesh/ — 2026-09-23, already cited there). This product's own spec already states "No card or wallet credentials are ever stored" (`docs/spec/fees-and-alerts.md` §1) — consistent with standard PCI-DSS-avoidance practice (we never touch card data at all, so PCI-DSS scope is minimal), but this is a design decision already made in the spec, not new legal research from this task; flagged as consistent, not verified against Bangladesh Bank's specific PSD circulars, which this search did not retrieve directly.

### 1.5 National ID / birth-registration data
- `docs/spec/domain-model.md` §1 already states "No national ID or birth-registration number is stored in v1 (data minimisation)." This is a design decision, not a legal requirement confirmed by this task's research, but it is consistent with the general caution seen in the sources above about the sensitivity of NID-linked data in Bangladesh (one PDPA-era report above discusses a separate incident of leaked NID-linked voter data). Not independently verified against Bangladesh's National Identity Registration Act — **unknown**, flag for the lawyer.

### 1.6 What this task did not find
- The full text of the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 itself (only secondary summaries/trackers were retrieved).
- Any Bangladesh-specific rule on data retention periods for a specific sector like education.
- Any existing Bangladeshi enforcement action, fine, or case specifically involving a school-management or SaaS product, to calibrate real-world risk.

## 3. Checklist

### Group A — must do before pilot
1. **Guardian consent for minors' data**: a clear, opt-in consent step at admission/enrolment (paper or digital) covering what data is collected about the student and why, consistent with the PDPA's parent/guardian consent requirement for children's data (§1.1).
2. **Privacy notice content drafted** (by a lawyer, per M4-L, not by this task) but the *inputs* to it should include: what is collected (§2 above), why, who sees it, how long it's kept, and how a guardian can ask for correction/deletion.
3. **No card or wallet credentials stored** — already true per `fees-and-alerts.md` §1; keep this constraint explicit in any future spec change.
4. **Text-message opt-out mechanism** — `student_guardians.receive_alerts` already exists as a per-guardian flag; confirm there is a real, discoverable way for a guardian to ask staff to turn it off (a UI toggle is ideal but not yet specified — a Requests item below).
5. **Staff access scoped by role** — confirm (via `permissions.md`, not read by this task) that teachers cannot see guardian phone numbers or addresses they don't need, consistent with data-minimisation.
6. **Basic breach response plan** (informal, pre-launch): who gets notified internally if something is exposed, even though Bangladeshi law does not currently mandate notifying affected people (§1.1) — doing it anyway is good practice and likely expected by institution customers even if not legally required.

### Group B — must do before launch
7. **Formal retention and deletion policy** — for each table in §2, a stated retention period and a deletion/anonymisation process when a student leaves or a guardian asks; currently the spec has soft-delete (`deleted_at`) on `students` and `guardians` but no stated retention *period* — this is a gap to close before launch, not before pilot.
8. **Cross-border storage review** — confirm with a lawyer whether any of our stored fields (especially `students.date_of_birth`, `photo_path`, `address`) fall under the PDPA's "critical" data category that may require in-country storage, given D-10 hosts the database outside Bangladesh (§1.1's biggest open question).
9. **Sub-processor list** — Supabase (database/auth/storage), Netlify (hosting), the eventual SMS provider (`M0-R2`), the eventual payment aggregator (`M0-R2`), and any monitoring tool (Sentry) are all sub-processors touching personal data to some degree; a lawyer-reviewed list with each one's role and data-location should exist before launch.
10. **Backups** — confirm backup retention (Supabase's bundled daily backups, per `cost-model.md`) doesn't silently outlive the primary-data retention policy from item 7 (a deleted student's data could still exist in a backup for the backup's retention window — this should be disclosed, not hidden).
11. **Audit-log retention policy** — because `audit_log.before`/`after` can contain a full copy of personal data (§2), decide explicitly how long audit rows are kept and who can read them, rather than treating this as "just logs."
12. **BTRC promotional-vs-transactional SMS classification** — confirm with a lawyer or BTRC directly whether attendance/fee/result alerts count as "transactional" (not subject to the Bengali-only promotional-SMS rule) so template language choices (Bangla vs bilingual) are made deliberately.

### Group C — nice to have
13. A guardian-facing "what we collect and why" summary page in the portal (plain-language, not the full legal privacy notice) — improves trust and digital literacy given the low-tech-confidence audience noted in `fees-and-alerts.md` §6.
14. A yearly internal review of whether each optional/nullable personal-data field (gender, date of birth, address, occupation — all flagged `Maybe`/`Yes candidate` in §2) is actually being used by any real institution, to periodically re-apply data minimisation rather than deciding once and forgetting.
15. A public-facing security/trust page once M4-L's Terms/Privacy Policy exist, summarising practices like "no card data stored," "RLS-enforced tenant isolation," and the sub-processor list, in plain language for institution decision-makers who are evaluating us against competitors (see `docs/research/competitors.md`).

## 4. Questions for a lawyer (at least 10)

1. Under the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026, does any field we store about students or guardians (especially date of birth, address, or photo) count as data "critical to national security" or otherwise subject to mandatory in-Bangladesh storage, given our database is hosted outside Bangladesh per D-10?
2. What specific form must "parent or legal guardian consent" for a minor's data take under the PDPA to be legally valid — is a checkbox at admission enough, or is a signed physical form or something more required?
3. Since the PDPA is reported to have no mandatory breach-notification requirement, are we nonetheless exposed to liability under the Cyber Security Act 2023 or general tort/contract law if we fail to notify affected guardians after a breach?
4. Does the BTRC's Bengali-only rule for "promotional" SMS apply to our attendance/fee/result alerts, which we consider transactional, or could a regulator classify them as promotional?
5. What is the legally required or customer-expected minimum retention period for academic records (marks, attendance, results) in Bangladesh, separate from our own data-minimisation preference to delete data sooner?
6. When a student leaves an institution, what is the legally safest process for the resulting guardian/student personal data — full deletion, anonymisation, or retention for a defined period for academic-record purposes?
7. Does using Supabase (a foreign-owned, foreign-hosted processor) and Netlify as sub-processors create any specific contractual or disclosure obligation to guardians under the PDPA's cross-border transfer rules?
8. Is a single, generic consent captured at admission sufficient for all future processing (attendance, results, fee alerts, notices), or does the PDPA's purpose-limitation principle require separate, purpose-specific consents?
9. Given `audit_log` can contain full copies of personal data in `before`/`after` columns, does a "right to deletion" request from a guardian legally require us to also purge or anonymise historical audit-log entries, or can audit trails be legitimately exempted for compliance/security purposes?
10. What is our actual legal exposure (fines, criminal liability under the PDPA's reported penalty ranges) if we operate through the pilot phase before finalising a lawyer-drafted Privacy Policy and Terms of Service (M4-L is scheduled after this task, not before a pilot)?
11. Does storing a guardian's `occupation` (currently collected per §2's data inventory) create any additional data-sensitivity obligation, or should we simply drop the field as unnecessary under data-minimisation, from a legal-risk perspective rather than a product one?
12. Are there Bangladesh Bank rules specific to storing a payment `reference` (e.g. a bKash transaction ID) as opposed to card/wallet credentials, that we should be aware of even though we do not store the credentials themselves?

## Task-specific items

  - Whether the PDPA's "18 months after gazette" phase-in (reported for the 2025 Ordinance, implying ~May 2027) still applies to obligations under the now-enacted 2026 Act, or whether the Act reset that clock — not resolved by this search, **unknown**.
  - The exact definition and criteria for data "critical to national security" under the PDPA (relevant to D-10 cross-border hosting) — only described in general, second-hand terms by a civil-society commentary source, not found in a primary legal text in this search.
